## What India VIX means

**India VIX** is a volatility index published by NSE (the National Stock Exchange of India). It is computed from the prices of Nifty index options, using the best bid and ask quotes of out-of-the-money options in the near and mid-month contracts. NSE describes it as indicating "the investor's perception of the market's volatility in the near term": the **expected volatility over the next 30 calendar days**. Higher values mean higher expected volatility; lower values, lower.

Three things it is *not*: it is not the Nifty's price, it is not a measure of how much the market *has* moved, and it is not a signal about which way the market will go. It is a number backed out of option prices.

"VIX" is short for volatility index, and the name is a CBOE (Chicago Board Options Exchange) trademark licensed to NSE. This post is part of [Jargon, Decoded]({{ '/series/jargon/' | relative_url }}). It describes the index and does not give a live reading or suggest acting on one.

## How it is built, in plain steps

From NSE's [computation methodology](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/India_VIX_comp_meth.pdf) and [white paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/white_paper_IndiaVIX.pdf) (both undated NSE documents, read on 1 November 2026):

1. NSE takes the latest price of the Nifty futures contract for each expiry as the forward index level.
2. The strike just below that level is the at-the-money strike. Out-of-the-money calls (strikes above it) and puts (strikes below it) are used; their best bid and ask quotes feed the calculation.
3. Where quotes are missing, values are filled in by a statistical method called a natural cubic spline.
4. Variance (volatility squared) is computed separately for the near and mid-month expiry, giving each option a weight that rises with its average bid-ask quote and falls with its strike price.
5. The two variances are interpolated to a single value for a constant 30 days to expiry.
6. The square root of that variance is multiplied by 100.

NSE says the method follows CBOE's, adapted to the Nifty options order book, and that the time to expiry is measured in minutes and the risk-free rate is the NSE MIBOR for the matching tenure.

## The formula

```
India VIX  =  σ × 100        (σ is the 30-day expected volatility,
                                expressed as an annual rate)

σ  =  √( interpolated variance of near-month and mid-month option prices )
```

The white paper says the index "is denoted as an annualised percentage". So a VIX of 16 means σ = 0.16 *per year*, not a 16% move in a month. To get the implied size of a typical move over 30 days you scale by the square root of time:

```
Implied one-standard-deviation move over 30 days  =  VIX × √(30 ÷ 365)   (in % of the index level)
```

Think of the weather forecast saying "very stormy week ahead" or "calm week ahead". The forecast isn't the rain itself; it's what the forecasters *expect*. India VIX is like that for the market's ups and downs: it is how bumpy the people trading options *expect* the next month to be, worked out from the prices they're willing to pay. A high number means they expect a bumpy ride. It doesn't say whether the bumps go up or down, and the forecast can be wrong.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading it as a direction signal.** A high VIX means options are priced for large moves, in either direction. Phrases like "the VIX says buy" or "the VIX says sell" read a direction into a number that has none.
- **Reading it as a forecast of what will happen.** It reflects option prices today. Actual moves can be calmer or wilder than the number implies.
- **Forgetting it is annualised.** A VIX of 16 is not a 16% swing in the next month; scale it by the square root of time, as above.
- **Confusing it with realised volatility.** Realised volatility is measured from past returns (the Sharpe post's σ). India VIX is implied by option prices and looks forward. The two can differ for long stretches.

**Takeaway:** India VIX is NSE's index of expected Nifty volatility over the next 30 calendar days, backed out of option prices and quoted as an annualised percentage. A higher number means bigger expected swings in either direction, and nothing about which way.
