## What ROIC means

**ROIC, return on invested capital,** asks what the business earns, after
tax, on the money actually tied up in running it. It uses operating profit
after tax, called NOPAT (net operating profit after tax), and divides it by
invested capital: what shareholders and lenders have put in, *minus* the cash
the business is just holding.

That last step is the difference from [ROCE]({% post_url 2026-09-03-roce %})
(return on capital employed), which counts all of equity and borrowings,
cash included. For a company sitting on a pile of cash, the two can tell
quite different stories, as Desi Bites shows. ROIC has no single official
definition, so this post states the one it uses. It's part of
[Jargon, Decoded]({{ '/series/jargon/' | relative_url }}).

## The formula

EBIT is earnings before interest and tax: operating profit, before other
income.

```
NOPAT             =  EBIT × (1 − tax rate)
Invested capital  =  equity + borrowings − cash
ROIC              =  NOPAT ÷ average invested capital

For comparison:
ROCE              =  EBIT ÷ average (equity + borrowings)        (pre-tax, cash included)
```

The tax rate here is the year's
[effective rate]({% post_url 2026-12-04-effective-tax-rate %}): tax ÷
profit before tax. Averages use the opening and closing balances, as the
ROCE post does.

its invested capital than that capital costs is adding value, the idea the

work through.
Whether Desi Bites clears that bar isn't assessed here.

## Common mistakes

1. **Comparing ROIC with a pre-tax ROCE.** ROIC is after tax; ROCE is usually
   before. Put them on the same basis first, as the table's last row does.
2. **Assuming everyone defines invested capital the same way.** Some subtract
   all cash, some only "excess" cash; some take out goodwill, some don't (it's
   in here). Check the definition before comparing two sources' figures.
3. **Reading one year's ROIC as the business's level.** An acquisition or a
   big capex year moves the denominator before the profit arrives. Look at
   several years.

**Takeaway:** ROIC divides after-tax operating profit by the capital actually
invested in the business, leaving idle cash out, so it shows what the operating
business earns. Next to ROCE, it separates operating returns from the drag of
cash held.
